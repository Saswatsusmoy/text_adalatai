Assignment pillar 1: measure and reduce Indic token inefficiency. We compared tokenizer families on the legal EN-HI pairs, trained domain SentencePiece models, froze a production SPM for Track C experiments, and tested whether vocab-extending NLLB improves translation (it did not, on this budget -- see Track C).

What was measured

  • Hindi chars per token (higher = denser packing)
  • HI / EN token ratio (closer to 1 = less Indic overhead vs English)
  • Total tokens on the corpus (proxy for context length and cost)
  • Whether Devanagari exists as first-class pieces or falls back to UTF-8 bytes

Code: src/tokenizer/benchmark.py, deep_dive.py. JSON dumps under data/analysis/tokenizer_*.json.

Family-level findings

FamilyExamplesHindi on legal text
Char-aware SentencePiece Unigram domain SPM, Gemma-class SP Strong -- Devanagari first-class
Multilingual BPE with Dev pieces NLLB, OpenAI o200k Good packing
Byte-level BPE many Llama-line models Weak -- often 0 Dev vocab entries; UTF-8 byte split; ~1.1-2.7x HI cost
Important distinction

The failure mode is byte-level / Devanagari-blind BPE, not "all BPE". SentencePiece model_type=bpe is still Unicode-aware. Domain work uses Unigram SP.

Integration strategy

TrackIdeaOutcome
D production Keep NLLB native tokenizer; adapt model with LoRA Shipped (A2)
C science Domain SPM + model adapt / vocab-extend C0 freeze done; C1c quality fail on dual policy

Why production keeps NLLB tokens: packing wins from domain SPM do not automatically become quality wins after short LoRA. Vocab surgery without long embedding warm-up lost dual-policy quality (Track C pages). For legal fidelity, LoRA on stock NLLB won.
