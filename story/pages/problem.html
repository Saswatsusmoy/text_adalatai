Court judgments are a hard EN->HI case: long sentences, archaic legal English, mixed-script Hindi (Devanagari plus English citations like S.C.R., Section, Article), and domain terms that generic web MT under-serves. The assignment therefore forces two linked questions -- how expensive is Hindi to tokenize, and can a small model adapt to legal style without full fine-tune cost.

Why two pillars

  • Token efficiency controls sequence length, context budget, and inference cost under LLMs and NMT tokenizers.
  • Domain adaptation controls fidelity of legal meaning (party roles, constitutional articles, procedure).

Optimizing only packing can ship a tokenizer story that does not translate better. Optimizing only BLEU without dual holdouts can overfit a 30-document set. This project measures both.

Hard constraints (frozen)

  • No re-split of assignment document IDs after freeze.
  • SPM train text may use Stage A + assignment train only -- never assignment dev/test docs.
  • Stage A MT train excludes Policy E holdouts (stage_a_train.jsonl only).
  • Zero-shot baseline before claiming LoRA gains.
  • Final claims use fixed decode protocol: beam 4, max_new 256, max_in 256.
  • Every MT system reports Policy I_test, E_milpac_test, E_anuvaad_test.
